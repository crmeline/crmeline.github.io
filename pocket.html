A banking app that makes kids actually want to manage their money

[Overview]

A banking app for kids aged 10 to 17. Most teen banking apps feel like shrunken adult apps, so I wanted to design one that teaches real money habits and still feels fun to use.

[Problem]

Teen banking apps are functional but dry. They assume a 12 year old already cares about savings goals and transaction history, so it ends up feeling like homework.

[Solution]

The app covers the basics like sending money, QR payments, transactions, and savings goals. On top of that, saving, donating, and hitting milestones earns XP and levels up your profile, with a leaderboard to compete with friends. The home screen shows a daily money tip next to your balance, so there’s always a reason to open it.

Design Decisions

[Dark mode with light colors]

Instead of the usual bright background, I used dark with purple, orange, green, and red accents. It feels more like a game than a bank, and the illustrated characters pop.

[Gamification that means something]

You level up by donating and hitting savings milestones, not just by logging in. Avatar borders change as you level, which gives kids something visible to work toward.

[Savings as pockets]

Each goal gets its own card and progress bar, so kids can see exactly how close they are to the thing they actually want.

[Donations with urgency]

Campaigns show a deadline and a progress bar, which makes giving feel real instead of abstract.

Learnings

Designing for kids is mostly about removing assumptions. Asking “would a 12 year old get this right away?” ruled out a lot of adult finance patterns and pushed me toward a more expressive look than I’d normally go for.

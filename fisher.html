Fisher

Oct 2025

A focus timer that gives you something to collect

[Overview]

Focus Fisher is a personal project I made for myself. It’s a focus timer where finishing a session means catching a fish. I skipped the full research process and treated it as a design exercise, to see if a little gamification could make a focus app worth coming back to.

[Problem]

Most focus apps do the job, but once the novelty wears off, nothing pulls you back. There’s no small reward for finishing a hard session, so for students who already struggle to stay consistent, it’s easy to stop opening the app.

[Solution]

After each session, you catch a fish. Fish come in different rarities, so there’s a little surprise in finding out what you got. The rest of the app stays quiet, with three tabs and muted colors, so the question was how to make it rewarding without turning it into another distraction.

Design Decisions

[Just three tabs]

Focus, My Fish, and Achievements. Anything that didn’t fit those three got cut.

[Reward after, not during]

Fish only appear once the timer ends, so the focus screen stays clean and the reward is tied to finishing the work.

[Rarity worth collecting]

Different tiers give you a reason to come back for the next session, not just the first one.

Learnings

This was a one week project with no brief, so every decision came down to one question, does this support focus or distract from it? That made it easy to cut features and kept the look consistent. I’d set a constraint like that earlier on future projects, even when there is a brief.
